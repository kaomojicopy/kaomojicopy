What is kaomoji?

Kaomoji (顔文字) are Japanese-style faces made from text characters, like (˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶) or (ﾉ◕ヮ◕)ﾉ*:･ﾟ✧. This guide explains what they are, where they come from, and how to use them well.

Kaomoji meaning

The word kaomoji joins two Japanese words: kao, meaning face, and moji, meaning character. So kaomoji means face characters. The faces are drawn with letters, punctuation, and symbols, which is why you can type or paste them anywhere text is allowed.

Kaomoji, kaomojis or kamoji?

Kaomoji is the standard spelling, and it is used for both one face and many. English speakers often add an s and write kaomojis, and some people type kamoji by mistake. All three spellings point to the same thing, so you can use whichever feels natural.

Where kaomoji came from

Kaomoji grew out of early Japanese online communities and text messaging, where people wanted to show feeling with the characters they already had. Japanese writing offers many extra characters, including katakana, which is why kaomoji can look so rich and detailed.

How kaomoji are built

A typical kaomoji has a pair of eyes, a mouth, and sometimes arms or decorations. Each part is a character. For example, ◕ can be an eye, ᴗ a mouth, and ♡ a heart beside the face. Because most of the feeling sits in the eyes, kaomoji read upright instead of sideways, unlike Western emoticons such as :).

Kaomoji symbols: the characters behind the faces

Kaomoji symbols come from many parts of Unicode, the standard that defines text characters. You will find katakana such as ヮ and ツ, math and shape symbols like ◕ and ▽, accent marks, and small decorations like ♡ and ✧. Because these are all text, the faces copy and paste like any other word.

Kaomoji vs emoticons vs emoji vs ASCII art

  • Emoticons are short text faces like :) that you read sideways.
  • Kaomoji are Japanese-style text faces that you read upright.
  • Emoji are small pictures from the Unicode standard, and they look different on each device.
  • ASCII art uses text to draw bigger pictures, and kaomoji are tiny, one-line versions of it.

Famous kaomoji

Some kaomoji became internet classics. You can copy the shrug emoticon, the lenny face, and the table flip from their own pages, or browse the meme faces. For sweeter styles, visit cute kaomoji and aesthetic kaomoji.

How to use kaomoji well

  • Pick a face that fits the tone of your message.
  • Use one face at a time so it stays easy to read.
  • Save your favorites so they are quick to find.
  • Remember that screen readers may read every symbol aloud, so do not rely on a kaomoji for important meaning.
