What is kaomoji?

Kaomoji (顔文字) are Japanese-style faces made from text characters, like (˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶) or (ﾉ◕ヮ◕)ﾉ*:･ﾟ✧. The word combines the Japanese words for "face" and "character". Unlike emoji, which are tiny pictures, kaomoji are built from letters, punctuation, and symbols, so you can paste them anywhere you can type text.

How to copy and paste kaomoji

Find a face by mood or search, then tap it. It is copied to your clipboard right away. Paste it with Ctrl+V (or Cmd+V on a Mac), or long-press in a text box on your phone and choose Paste. Tap the heart on a face to keep it in your favorites.

Where can you use kaomoji?

In chats and direct messages, social media comments and bios, forum posts, emails, notes, and documents. Most apps that accept plain text will accept kaomoji too, though a few faces may look slightly different depending on the fonts on your device.

Kaomoji, kaomojis and kamoji: which spelling is right?

The standard spelling is kaomoji, and it works for one face or many. Plenty of people write kaomojis with an English plural, and some type kamoji by mistake. They all mean the same thing: Japanese-style faces made from text. If you searched for any of these spellings, you are in the right place.

The word comes from Japanese. Kao means face and moji means character, so kaomoji literally means face characters. You can read more in our guide, what is kaomoji.

Kaomoji, emoticons and emoji: what is the difference?

Western emoticons such as :) are read sideways, and emoji are small pictures that look different on each device. Kaomoji are Japanese emoticons that you read upright, and they put most of the feeling in the eyes, like (ᵔ◡ᵔ). Because kaomoji are plain text, they paste the same way everywhere.

For a deeper comparison, including ASCII art and symbols, see Japanese emoticons explained.

Find the right kaomoji for your message

Start with the mood. Cute kaomoji are best for soft, friendly messages, and happy kaomoji cover smiles, cheers, and good news. For affection, try love kaomoji and heart emoticons. Feeling low? Sad kaomoji have tears and sighs, and angry faces cover frustration and pouts.

Looking for the classics? Copy the shrug emoticon, the lenny face, or the table flip, or browse the meme faces for the facepalm and more. Fans of decoration will like the aesthetic kaomoji with stars, sparkles, and flowers, and animal lovers can browse cat kaomoji, bear kaomoji, and bunny kaomoji.

Tips for using kaomoji in messages

  • Match the tone. A soft face suits a thank-you, and a bold one suits a joke.
  • Use one face at a time. A single kaomoji reads clearly, while a long chain can look messy.
  • Put it at the end of a sentence, or send it alone as a quick reaction.
  • Keep faces short in places with limited space, like bios and usernames.
  • Test an unusual face in your app first, since a few symbols may look different on some devices.

Why plain-text faces still work so well

Kaomoji need no app, no keyboard download, and no special font. You copy them, paste them, and they work in chats, comments, emails, notes, and documents. They can also say more than a single emoji, since a face can include arms, sparkles, and small objects. Questions

Is Kaomoji Copy free to use?

Yes. Every face is free to copy and paste, and there is nothing to sign up for.

How do I copy a kaomoji?

Tap or click any face. It is copied to your clipboard and a small message confirms it. Then paste it where you want it, with Ctrl+V on a computer or by long-pressing and choosing Paste on a phone.

What is the difference between kaomoji and emoji?

Emoji are small pictures. Kaomoji are faces built from ordinary text characters and symbols, and they are read upright instead of tilted on their side like :-).

Where are my favorites saved?

In your own browser, on the device you are using. There is no account, so favorites do not follow you to another device, and clearing your browser data removes them.

Why does a face look different or show empty boxes on my device?

Kaomoji use many different symbols, and each phone or computer draws them with its own fonts. If a symbol is missing from your device's fonts, it can look slightly different or show as a box. Another face from the same collection usually works fine.

Can I use kaomoji on my phone?

Yes. The site works on iPhone, Android, and desktop browsers. Tap a face to copy it, then paste it into any app that accepts text.

What does kaomoji mean?

Kaomoji is a Japanese word that combines kao, meaning face, and moji, meaning character. It describes faces built from text characters, such as (˶ᵔ ᵕ ᵔ˶).

Is it kaomoji, kaomojis or kamoji?

Kaomoji is the correct spelling and can be used for one face or many. Kaomojis is a common English plural, and kamoji is a frequent misspelling. All of them lead to the same kind of text faces.

What are the best kaomoji for Instagram and TikTok?

Cute, aesthetic, and happy kaomoji are the most popular for bios, captions, and comments. Copy one, paste it, and keep it short so it stays easy to read.

Are kaomoji the same as ASCII art?

They are related. ASCII art uses text characters to make pictures, and kaomoji are very small pictures of faces. Kaomoji are usually one line long and meant to be sent in messages.

Can I make my own kaomoji?

Yes. Combine characters you like for the eyes, mouth, and arms, and test the result in your app. You can start by copying a face from the library and changing one part.
