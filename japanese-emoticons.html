Japanese emoticons explained

Japanese emoticons, called kaomoji, are text faces that you read upright. This guide compares them with Western emoticons, smiley faces, ASCII art, and symbol sets, so you know which one to use.

What are Japanese emoticons?

Japanese emoticons are faces made from text characters, such as (^_^) or (ᵔ◡ᵔ). They are called kaomoji in Japanese, and you will also see them described as text faces or cute emoticons. They show feeling mostly through the eyes.

Japanese emoticons vs Western emoticons

Western emoticons, like :-) and :(, are written sideways and use only a few characters. Japanese emoticons are written upright and often use extra symbols for blush marks, arms, hearts, and sparkles. That makes them more expressive for the same amount of space.

Smiley faces and symbols

A smiley face can be a text emoticon, a Unicode symbol such as ☺, or an emoji picture. Symbols and emoticons are related, because kaomoji symbols are the building blocks of every face. A heart emoticon, for instance, uses ♡ or ♥ next to a face. If you want a ready-made smile, try the happy kaomoji page. Our guide to kaomoji symbols lists the characters and their Unicode names.

ASCII art and text faces

ASCII art draws pictures with text, sometimes across many lines. Kaomoji are a small, single-line cousin of ASCII art. They are easier to send in messages and easier to read at a glance.

Popular emoticon styles

  • Cute emoticons with sparkly eyes and blushing cheeks
  • Heart emoticons for hugs, kisses, and love notes
  • Star emoticons and sparkles for a dreamy look
  • Cat emoticons with whiskers and paws
  • Shrug emoticons, lenny faces, and table flips
